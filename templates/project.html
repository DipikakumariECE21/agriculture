Abstract

Crop diseases are a major cause of reduced agricultural productivity and economic loss. This project proposes a technology-assisted system that identifies visible crop diseases from images of plant leaves: capture an image, preprocess it, extract visual features and classify the leaf as healthy or affected by a particular disease.

It shows how image processing, artificial intelligence, electronics and communication technologies combine to support agriculture — reducing dependence on visual inspection alone, giving faster preliminary detection and encouraging timely crop management.

Objectives

What the project set out to do.

  1. Develop a prototype for detecting visible crop diseases from leaf images.
  2. Understand image acquisition and preprocessing techniques.
  3. Apply machine-learning and deep-learning concepts to agricultural images.
  4. Provide a simple, understandable disease prediction output.
  5. Reduce the time required for preliminary visual screening.
  6. Demonstrate electronics, computing and communication technologies in agriculture.

Advantages

  • Quick preliminary screening
  • Consistent, computer-based classification
  • Scalable to additional crops and diseases
  • Integrates with mobile and IoT platforms
  • Supports remote agricultural assistance

Limitations

Accuracy depends on dataset quality, disease coverage and image conditions. A model trained on laboratory images may not perform as well on complex field photos, and similar-looking diseases are hard to tell apart. Patra is therefore a decision-support and screening tool, not a final agricultural diagnosis.

Applications

  • Smart agriculture and precision farming
  • Mobile crop-health assistance
  • Agricultural research and education
  • Nursery and greenhouse monitoring
  • Remote advisory platforms

Future scope

Where it goes next.

With enough real-world data the system can move from a classroom prototype to field-level deployment.
